# QA Engineer / SDET — Role Overview: The Engineers Who Decide If Software Is Ready to Ship

## What QA and SDET Actually Mean — and Why They Are Not the Same Thing

Quality Assurance Engineer and Software Development Engineer in Test are two distinct roles that Indian job postings frequently conflate, and the confusion costs candidates real opportunities. Understanding the difference is the first step to positioning yourself correctly in this career.

A traditional QA Engineer is focused on the discipline of software quality — designing test plans, writing test cases, executing tests (manual and automated), filing detailed bug reports, and ensuring that software meets its functional and non-functional requirements before it ships to users. In many Indian IT service companies — TCS, Infosys, Wipro — QA engineers spend a significant portion of their time on manual testing, particularly for large enterprise systems where automated coverage is limited or where business logic is complex enough to require human judgment for validation.

An SDET — Software Development Engineer in Test — is fundamentally a software engineer who specializes in testability and quality infrastructure. SDETs do not primarily execute test cases. They build the frameworks, tools, and pipelines that allow the entire engineering team to test effectively and efficiently. At companies like Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and increasingly at Indian product companies like Razorpay, PhonePe, and Flipkart, SDETs write sophisticated automation frameworks, build CI/CD pipeline integrations, create test environments, develop mocking and stubbing libraries, and design observability tooling. An SDET's primary output is code, not test reports.

The industry in India has been moving clearly and consistently toward the SDET model for the past five years. Manual QA roles are shrinking at product companies. Automation engineering roles — and the SDET title — are growing. Engineers who see QA as a career path should understand this shift not as a threat, but as an opportunity: the bar for manual QA is declining, but the demand for engineers who can build reliable automation infrastructure is growing faster than the supply.

Why does any of this matter for users of HireNexus? Because if you are preparing for a QA career, the skills you invest in determine which type of role you will land. Investing primarily in manual testing skills will get you opportunities at service companies and enterprise clients. Investing in programming, automation frameworks, and CI/CD will get you opportunities at product companies with significantly higher compensation. Both paths are valid — but they need to be chosen consciously, not by default.

## The Real Job Description Across Seniority Levels

The daily work of a QA engineer shifts dramatically with seniority, and understanding this progression helps you set concrete goals for each stage of your career.

At the junior level (0–2 years), a QA engineer's day is typically: 40–50% writing and executing test cases for features currently in development, 20–30% reporting and triaging bugs in collaboration with developers, 15% participating in sprint ceremonies (standups, sprint planning, retrospectives), and 10–15% maintaining existing test scripts. At this stage, the most important skill is not any particular tool — it is the ability to think adversarially about software. Senior engineers constantly marvel at how many junior QA engineers test only the happy path. The happy path is what the developer already tested. Your job is to find every way the system can break: invalid inputs, empty states, network failures, concurrent users, edge cases the developer never considered.

At the mid-level (2–5 years), the SDET track and the manual QA track diverge noticeably. Mid-level SDETs are spending 50–60% of their time writing automation code — Selenium, Cypress, Playwright, or Appium scripts for UI automation; RestAssured or Karate for API automation; JUnit or TestNG frameworks for unit and integration tests. They are also taking ownership of the team's automation suite: maintaining it, improving its reliability, extending its coverage, and integrating it into the CI/CD pipeline so tests run automatically on every pull request.

At the senior level (5+ years), the job is almost entirely engineering and strategy. Senior SDETs design the overall testing architecture for a product or platform: which layers to test at (unit, integration, contract, E2E), how to structure the test pyramid, how to build a test environment that mirrors production closely enough to be meaningful, how to measure test effectiveness with metrics like defect escape rate and automation coverage. Senior QA leads at companies like Atlassian, Adobe, Freshworks, and Zoho also manage quality processes across multiple teams — coaching developers on writing better unit tests, reviewing automation code, and influencing how the engineering organization thinks about quality.

## Why Quality Engineering Is Undervalued and Why That Is Changing

Quality engineering occupies an unusual position in the Indian tech ecosystem. Among engineering students, it is rarely a first choice — most CS graduates want to be developers, and QA is seen as a backup or a stepping stone. This perception is outdated, inaccurate, and increasingly costly to those who hold it.

The practical reality is that product companies have learned, through painful production incidents, that poor test coverage is expensive. Every hour of engineering time spent debugging a production issue is time not spent building features. Every public outage is a reputation cost. Every data corruption bug in a fintech app is potentially a regulatory incident. Companies like Zepto, CRED, Groww, and PhonePe — where software reliability directly determines business outcomes — have learned to invest heavily in quality infrastructure. The engineers who build that infrastructure are compensated accordingly.

The other force driving demand for quality engineers is the complexity of modern software systems. A monolithic application with a few hundred APIs is testable with a modest automation suite. But a microservices architecture with hundreds of services, multiple databases, event queues, and external API dependencies is exponentially harder to test correctly. The SDET role exists precisely because this complexity requires dedicated engineering attention — not just a developer writing a few unit tests in their spare time.

For Indian engineers considering this career path: the SDET role at top Indian product companies and at MNC engineering centres in Hyderabad and Bengaluru commands salaries that are fully competitive with SDE roles at the same level. The perception gap — where SDE is seen as higher-status — is a cultural artifact that does not reflect the actual compensation data. An SDET-3 at Microsoft Hyderabad or a Staff QA Engineer at Atlassian Bengaluru earns as much as their developer counterparts.

## What Companies Actually Measure in QA/SDET Roles

Across all levels and company types, quality engineers are evaluated on a consistent set of dimensions that do not always match what the job posting says.

**Test coverage that actually finds bugs.** The most important metric is not the number of test cases written — it is the defect escape rate: how many bugs reach production that your tests should have caught. QA engineers who write thousands of shallow tests that never find real bugs are less valuable than engineers who write focused, adversarial tests targeting the highest-risk code paths. Senior interviewers at product companies often evaluate candidates specifically on their instinct for risk — their ability to identify which parts of the system are most likely to break under real-world conditions.

**Code quality in automation.** Automation code is software. It requires the same care as production code: readable naming, proper abstraction, test isolation, version control, and documentation. QA engineers who write fragile, tightly-coupled automation scripts that break every time a developer changes a CSS class or an API response format create more work than they save. Companies evaluate SDET candidates on the quality of their automation code, not just whether it runs.

**Collaboration with developers.** The most effective quality engineers are not gatekeepers who catch bugs after the fact — they are partners who shift quality left, working with developers during the design phase to identify testability concerns, reviewing code for potential edge cases, and helping developers write better unit tests. QA engineers who are perceived as adversarial or bureaucratic have less career mobility than those who are seen as collaborative quality partners.

# QA Engineer / SDET Skill Roadmap: From Test Cases to Test Platforms

## Phase 1 — Foundations That Every QA Engineer Must Have (Month 1–4)

The mistake most junior QA aspirants make is starting with a testing tool — downloading Selenium, watching a tutorial, and calling themselves an automation engineer after writing three scripts. Tools are not skills. The underlying skills are what matter, and without them, your automation will be brittle, your tests will be unreliable, and your career growth will plateau within two years.

The first foundational skill is programming. You must be able to write real code — not just copy-paste automation snippets. The recommended language for QA automation in the Indian market is Java (most enterprise automation frameworks are Java-based) or Python (increasingly popular at product companies and in API testing). Pick one and go deep. You need to understand object-oriented programming principles — classes, inheritance, encapsulation, polymorphism — because every serious automation framework is built around OOP concepts. You need to understand data structures well enough to write scripts that process test data intelligently. And you need to understand exception handling, file I/O, and basic debugging before you write a single line of automation code.

The second foundational skill is software testing theory. This sounds obvious, but it is genuinely overlooked. Before you automate anything, you need to understand test design techniques: equivalence partitioning, boundary value analysis, decision table testing, state transition testing, and pairwise testing. These techniques determine where you focus your test coverage — without them, you waste effort testing things that rarely break and miss the edges where defects actually live. The ISTQB Foundation Level certification (available in India through multiple authorized training providers) is a structured way to build this knowledge and is recognized by hiring managers at large service companies. It is not a substitute for practical skill, but it is a useful framework for the fundamentals.

The third foundational area is understanding how software is built — not in depth, but enough to understand what you are testing. Learn how a web application works: client-server architecture, HTTP request-response cycle, REST APIs, databases, and what happens between a user clicking a button and data appearing on screen. This knowledge is what separates QA engineers who can write meaningful test cases from those who test only the surface. When you understand that a form submission involves client-side validation, an HTTP POST request, server-side business logic validation, a database write, and a response — you can test each layer independently and identify which layer a bug lives in.

## Phase 2 — Core Automation Skills (Month 4–9)

Once your foundations are solid, build your core automation toolkit. The goal of this phase is to become genuinely proficient — not just familiar — with the tools that appear in the majority of Indian SDET job descriptions.

For UI automation, the market has shifted significantly in the last three years. Selenium WebDriver was the dominant tool for a decade and is still widely used at enterprise and service companies. But product companies are increasingly adopting Playwright and Cypress because they are faster, more reliable (no flakiness from implicit waits), and offer better developer experience. The right investment in 2025 is to learn Playwright as your primary UI automation tool while understanding Selenium's architecture because you will encounter it in legacy codebases. Playwright's auto-waiting, network interception capabilities, and cross-browser support make it the most powerful UI automation option available today.

For API testing, learn to use Postman for manual API exploration — every QA engineer in India needs to be comfortable here — and then go deeper into automated API testing with RestAssured (if your primary language is Java) or the requests library combined with pytest (if you are using Python). API tests are faster, more reliable, and more maintainable than UI tests. A mature test suite should have the API layer as its primary automation layer, with UI automation reserved for end-to-end flows that cannot be verified at the API level.

For test framework structure, learn how to organize a test suite properly using Page Object Model for UI automation — where each page of the application is represented by a class that encapsulates that page's locators and actions. This is not optional. Automation suites that are not structured with POM or an equivalent abstraction pattern become unmaintainable within six months. Learn also how to write parameterized tests (data-driven testing), how to use test fixtures and setup/teardown properly, and how to generate test reports that non-engineers can read.

For mobile testing — especially relevant in India, where mobile-first applications dominate — learn Appium at a basic level. Appium is the industry standard for mobile automation and works with both Android and iOS. You do not need to be an Appium expert unless you are targeting companies with large mobile products, but familiarity with its architecture (it wraps the WebDriver protocol for native apps) and ability to write basic mobile automation scripts is a differentiator.

## Phase 3 — SDET-Level Skills: Infrastructure and Platform (Month 9–18)

This phase is what separates a test automation engineer from an SDET. If you want to work at top Indian product companies or MNC engineering centres, and if you want salaries in the ₹25–60 lakh range, you need to build skills that go beyond running test scripts.

CI/CD integration is the first critical skill at this level. Your automation is worth very little if it only runs locally on your laptop. Learn how to integrate your test suite into a CI/CD pipeline — specifically Jenkins (still the dominant CI tool at Indian companies), GitHub Actions (rapidly growing at startups and product companies), and GitLab CI. Learn how to configure pipeline stages, how to run tests in parallel to reduce total execution time, how to handle test failures in the pipeline and configure alerting, and how to generate and archive test reports automatically.

Containerization with Docker is increasingly required at senior SDET levels. Being able to run your test suite in a Docker container makes it fully portable and eliminates the "it works on my machine" problem that plagues many automation setups. Learn how to write a Dockerfile for a test project, how to use Docker Compose to spin up a test environment with dependencies (databases, mock services), and how to use containers in CI/CD pipelines.

Performance testing is a significant differentiator. Most QA engineers never learn it, which means those who do command a premium. Learn JMeter — the most common performance testing tool in Indian job descriptions — at a practical level: how to create test plans, parameterize requests with CSV data, define ramp-up profiles, configure assertions, and analyze results to identify throughput bottlenecks, response time degradation, and memory leaks. Learn also how to interpret the results and communicate performance findings to developers and architects in terms of specific bottlenecks rather than raw numbers.

Test data management and environment management are skills that senior SDETs develop through experience but can be accelerated through deliberate study. Understanding how to create isolated, reproducible test data — synthetic data that covers all required test scenarios without depending on specific production data — is what enables reliable automation at scale. Learn SQL at an intermediate level: you need to be able to query test databases, verify data changes, and set up test data programmatically.

# QA / SDET Interview Preparation: How to Clear Every Round at Every Level

## Understanding the Interview Structure at Different Company Types

QA and SDET interviews vary dramatically across company types, and preparing for the wrong type of interview is one of the most common mistakes candidates make. Service companies like TCS, Infosys, and Capgemini conduct competency-based interviews with standardized written tests covering testing concepts, basic programming, and situational scenarios. Product companies like Razorpay, Swiggy, Freshworks, and Zoho conduct technical interviews closer to developer interviews — with coding rounds, system design discussions, and deep automation architecture reviews. MNC engineering centres like Microsoft, Google, and Amazon conduct the most rigorous interviews in the industry, with multiple coding rounds, extensive system design, and behavioural interviews using the STAR methodology.

Prepare for the company type you are targeting, not for a generic QA interview. Too many candidates prepare with testing theory and basic Selenium and then walk into a Razorpay SDET interview expecting it to feel like a TCS QA interview. It will not. At product companies, you will be asked to write production-quality code on a whiteboard or shared editor, design an automation framework from scratch in a 45-minute conversation, and debug a broken test suite you have never seen before.

For service companies: Prepare ISTQB-aligned testing theory (test levels, test types, defect lifecycle, test documentation), basic SQL queries, and experience with at least one manual testing tool (JIRA, TestRail, or Zephyr for test case management). Prepare scenario-based answers: "How would you test a login page? A payment gateway? A search feature?" The interviewers are assessing your systematic thinking about test coverage, not your code.

For product companies and MNC centres: Prepare like an SDE would, with additional testing depth. You need data structures and algorithms (LeetCode Easy–Medium range is the minimum; Hard problems are expected at FAANG-level companies), automation framework design, API testing proficiency, and the ability to discuss your test strategy at a system level.

## The Coding Round: What to Expect and How to Prepare

SDET coding rounds at product companies are not significantly easier than SDE coding rounds. Companies like Microsoft, Adobe, and Atlassian — all of which have large SDET/QA engineering teams in India — test SDET candidates with the same DSA problems they use for developer hires. This surprises many QA candidates who expect the coding bar to be lower. It is not.

The topics you must cover: arrays and strings (essential — almost every interview has at least one), hashmaps and sets, linked lists, stacks and queues, trees (binary trees, BSTs — traversal algorithms are frequently tested), recursion and backtracking, and dynamic programming at the basic level (LeetCode easy DP problems). You do not need to be a competitive programmer to pass SDET coding rounds at most Indian product companies, but you need to be able to write clean, correct solutions to medium-difficulty problems within 30–40 minutes.

The additional testing-specific coding patterns you should be comfortable with: writing a function that parses a log file and extracts error counts; writing a script that validates JSON response structure against a schema; implementing a basic retry mechanism for flaky API calls; parsing CSV test data and parameterizing test execution. These are the patterns that appear in SDET-specific coding rounds that differ from SDE interviews.

## Automation Framework Design: The Round Most Candidates Fail

The automation framework design round is the most differentiating part of a senior SDET interview, and it is the round where the most candidates fail. The typical question sounds deceptively simple: "Design an automation framework for testing our web application." Or: "We have a mobile app with a REST backend — how would you structure the testing for it?" The question expects a structured, architectural answer, not a list of tools.

A strong framework design answer covers: the test pyramid (what percentage of tests should be unit, integration, and E2E, and why), the tools chosen for each layer and the rationale, how tests will be structured and organized, how test data will be managed (fixtures, factories, database seeding), how the framework will handle test environment setup and teardown, how tests will be integrated into CI/CD, how results will be reported, and how the framework will be maintained as the application grows.

The most common failure mode in this round is jumping to tools before establishing the strategy. Do not start your answer with "I would use Selenium for UI and RestAssured for API." Start with the risk model: "The highest-risk areas in any e-commerce system are the payment flow and inventory management, so I would prioritize test coverage there first, using contract tests between the payment service and the frontend, end-to-end tests for the critical payment paths, and performance tests for the inventory APIs under concurrent load." Then introduce tools as implementations of that strategy.

## Behavioural and Process Questions: The Underestimated Round

Every senior QA/SDET interview includes behavioural questions, and they are not soft filler — they are heavily weighted at companies like Amazon (which uses Leadership Principles) and Microsoft. The situations you must be prepared to discuss in the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result): a time when you found a critical bug late in the release cycle and had to make a judgment call about release readiness; a time when you disagreed with a developer about the severity of a defect; a time when your automation suite had significant flakiness and how you resolved it; a time when you advocated for a quality improvement that engineering leadership was initially reluctant to invest in; a time when you had to onboard a team to a new testing approach.

Prepare at least two stories for each of these scenarios with specific metrics — defect counts, automation coverage percentages, cycle time reductions, production incident rates before and after your intervention. Stories without metrics feel vague. Stories with metrics feel credible.

# QA / SDET Portfolio: What to Build, What to Show, and How to Present It

## Why QA Engineers Need a Portfolio (and Most Do Not Have One)

The majority of QA engineers in India do not have a public portfolio. They have work experience, some certifications, and a resume. This is fine for service company roles where hiring is volume-driven and credential-based. It is not fine for product company and MNC roles where hiring managers want to see how you think about quality before they invite you to an interview.

The reason portfolio is rare in QA is cultural: testing has traditionally been perceived as process work rather than creative work, and process work does not naturally produce artifacts you can show publicly. But SDET work produces exactly the kind of artifacts that can be publicly shared — automation frameworks, test infrastructure code, CI/CD configurations, performance test reports — and the SDETs who invest in building a visible public portfolio have a significant advantage over those who do not.

Your portfolio should live on GitHub. It should contain at minimum three repositories that demonstrate distinct dimensions of your skill. Each repository should have a comprehensive README that explains what the project tests, why you made the architecture decisions you made, how to run it, and what the results were. The README is often all a hiring manager reads before deciding whether to schedule an interview. Write it for a skeptical senior engineer who is deciding whether to spend 45 minutes on you.

## Project 1: An End-to-End Automation Framework for a Real Application

The most impactful portfolio project for any SDET is a well-structured automation framework that tests a real, publicly accessible application. Do not test a toy application you built yourself — that shows nothing except that you can write code that passes tests for code you wrote. Test a real, third-party application like a public e-commerce site, a publicly accessible API (OpenWeatherMap, Swiggy's public-facing APIs, GitHub's REST API), or an open-source application you can run locally.

The framework should demonstrate: Page Object Model for UI tests, data-driven testing with external test data, API test layer with schema validation, a CI/CD pipeline (GitHub Actions is ideal because it is visible in your GitHub profile), proper test reporting (Allure Report is the gold standard in India), and clear documentation of what is covered and what is not. The test coverage should not try to be exhaustive — a focused set of 40–60 tests that are stable, fast, and meaningful is more impressive than 400 tests that fail intermittently.

The code quality in this project will be evaluated as carefully as the testing logic. Use proper OOP design, meaningful variable and method names, no hardcoded values (all configuration in properties files or environment variables), proper exception handling, and meaningful assertions with clear failure messages. Every detail signals whether you write production-quality automation or tutorial-quality automation.

## Project 2: API Testing and Contract Testing Suite

A dedicated API testing project demonstrates a skill that is increasingly valued and surprisingly rare: the ability to test backend services at the API layer without a UI, and to validate that service contracts are maintained across releases.

Build a test suite against a public REST API — the JSONPlaceholder API, the GitHub REST API, or the Fake Store API are all good candidates. Your suite should cover: basic CRUD operation testing (GET, POST, PUT, DELETE with correct status code and response body assertions), schema validation (verifying that response structures match expected contracts, using JSON Schema or Pojo-based deserialization), error scenario testing (invalid inputs, missing required fields, authentication failures), and authentication testing (API key or OAuth token handling).

If you want to go further and truly differentiate yourself, add a consumer-driven contract testing layer using Pact. Contract testing is a concept that most mid-level QA engineers have heard of but very few have actually implemented. A portfolio that includes a working Pact consumer test demonstrates senior-level thinking about microservices testing that will get attention from hiring managers at companies with microservices architectures — which includes essentially every serious product company in India.

## Project 3: Performance Testing and Monitoring Project

Performance testing skills are explicitly listed in many senior QA job descriptions and rarely demonstrated in candidate portfolios. A project that shows you can design, execute, and interpret load tests is an immediate differentiator.

Build a JMeter or k6 test suite against a publicly accessible application or a locally hosted open-source app. The project should include: a baseline performance test that establishes expected response times and throughput, a load test that simulates a realistic number of concurrent users, a stress test that pushes beyond the expected load to find the breaking point, and a soak test that runs sustained load to identify memory leaks and resource exhaustion over time.

The most important part of this project is not the scripts — it is the analysis. Write a detailed report (even a README section) that interprets the results: at what concurrency level does response time degrade, where is the bottleneck (CPU, memory, database, network), and what are your recommendations? This analysis is what a real senior SDET would deliver to engineering leadership, and demonstrating it in your portfolio shows that you understand performance testing as an engineering discipline, not just a tool exercise.

# QA / SDET Job Market in India: Where the Opportunities Are and How to Find Them

## The Company Landscape for QA/SDET in India

India's QA and SDET job market is large, diverse, and bifurcated — the opportunities at the top and bottom of the market are so different that they almost constitute different careers. Understanding which tier you are targeting is essential for calibrating your preparation and expectations.

At the tier-1 level — MNC engineering centres — the SDET role is a first-class engineering position with compensation and career growth fully competitive with developer roles. Microsoft's India Development Centre in Hyderabad is one of the largest employers of SDET talent in the country; their SDET teams work on core products like Azure, Visual Studio, and Microsoft 365. Google Hyderabad and Bengaluru have Test Engineering teams working on Search, Maps, and YouTube. Amazon's India development centres in Hyderabad and Bengaluru have large SDET presence across AWS and consumer products. Atlassian Bengaluru is particularly notable in the Indian QA community for its strong quality engineering culture — Atlassian's SDET teams built some of the most sophisticated test infrastructure in the country. Adobe Noida and Bengaluru, Intuit Bengaluru, and Walmart Global Tech have similarly strong quality engineering cultures.

At the tier-2 level — Indian product companies — the quality engineering culture varies widely. Companies like Razorpay, Freshworks, Groww, PhonePe, Swiggy, Zepto, Cred, and Juspay have invested heavily in quality automation because their products handle real money and real user data where defects have immediate business consequences. Freshworks in particular has large SDET teams across Chennai and Bengaluru. Zoho, while known for its conservative culture, has strong quality processes and is a stable employer for quality engineers.

At the tier-3 level — IT service companies — QA remains primarily manual and process-driven. TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL, and Cognizant employ the largest number of QA professionals in India by headcount. The work is structured, the career path is predictable, and the compensation is modest compared to product companies. These companies are a reasonable entry point for freshers but typically not a destination for engineers who want to build SDET-level automation skills.

## Niche Markets and Emerging Opportunities

Beyond the established tiers, several niche areas are generating significant demand for specialized quality engineers in India.

Fintech testing is one of the highest-demand specializations. Companies like Razorpay, PayU, BillDesk, NiYO, and the banking software teams at TCS and Infosys need QA engineers who understand payment system testing — compliance testing, security testing, performance testing for high-volume transaction systems, and regulatory validation (RBI guidelines, PCI-DSS compliance testing). QA engineers with fintech domain knowledge command a 15–25% premium over generalist automation engineers.

Gaming QA is a small but growing niche in India, concentrated in Pune, Bengaluru, and Hyderabad. Companies like Zynga, Games2win, SuperGaming, and Nazara Technologies hire game testers and automation engineers with mobile testing specialization. The work is different from enterprise software testing — performance, compatibility across hundreds of device models, and user experience testing are the primary concerns — but it attracts engineers who want to work on consumer products with highly engaged user bases.

Cloud infrastructure testing and DevOps quality engineering is an emerging category at companies building cloud-native products and platforms. Engineers who combine SDET skills with DevOps knowledge — Kubernetes, Terraform, cloud provider services — are finding opportunities in platform engineering teams where they own the quality of the infrastructure itself, not just the applications running on it.

## How to Actually Find and Land QA/SDET Roles

The job search strategy for QA/SDET roles is different from developer job searches in important ways. LinkedIn is the primary channel for quality engineering roles in India — more so than for developer roles, where referrals and competitive coding platforms like HackerRank carry more weight. Build a LinkedIn profile that specifically calls out your automation tools, frameworks built, and the scale of systems you have tested. Hiring managers for SDET roles frequently use LinkedIn Recruiter to search for candidates by tool stack.

Testing communities are an underutilized channel. The Ministry of Testing (globally active, with an Indian chapter), the STC India testing community, and testing-specific meetups in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Chennai are places where both jobs and knowledge circulate. Engineers who are active in these communities — presenting at meetups, contributing to testing forums, writing about testing on blogs or LinkedIn — are consistently ahead of their peers in visibility to hiring managers.

Referrals at product companies work exactly the way they work for developers. If you want to work at Atlassian, Freshworks, or Razorpay, find a SDET currently employed there and ask for a referral. Referred candidates receive faster screening and higher conversion rates at every company in India.

# QA / SDET Salary Guide: What You Should Actually Be Earning at Every Level

## Fresher and Junior Level Salaries in India

Understanding the starting salary landscape for QA and SDET roles in India requires understanding the company tier, because the difference between service company and product company starting salaries is significant and growing.

At IT service companies — TCS, Infosys, Wipro, and their peers — QA engineer fresher salaries range from ₹3.5 to ₹5 lakh per annum. This reflects both the nature of the work (primarily manual testing in the first 1–2 years) and the supply-demand dynamics of these companies, which hire thousands of QA engineers annually and consequently have significant negotiating leverage over individuals. The growth trajectory from these starting points is predictable but slow — typical increments of 8–12% annually, with SDE-level promotions requiring 3–4 years of experience.

At mid-market Indian product companies — Freshworks, Zoho, Mphasis, Persistent, and similar — fresher automation engineers with programming skills and an automation project in their portfolio start at ₹7–12 lakh. The market recognizes that an automation engineer with genuine programming ability is fundamentally different from a manual QA, and the starting salary reflects this. Within 2–3 years, strong performers at these companies reach ₹18–28 lakh.

At top Indian product companies — Razorpay, PhonePe, CRED, Swiggy, Flipkart — SDET freshers with demonstrated coding ability and strong interview performance start at ₹18–28 lakh, fully competitive with SDE-1 offers. The companies that have invested in building quality engineering as a first-class discipline have aligned their QA/SDET compensation with their SDE compensation, because they understand that the engineering difficulty of SDET work is equivalent.

## Mid-Level and Senior Salaries

At the SDE-2 equivalent level (2–5 years), SDET salaries at Indian product companies range from ₹25–45 lakh. Engineers with strong automation framework experience, CI/CD integration skills, and a track record of building test infrastructure that meaningfully reduced production defect rates are at the higher end. The significant variance in this range is driven by company tier and the depth of the engineer's programming skills — SDETs who can be genuine contributors to a codebase (not just test code, but shared utilities, developer-facing testing tools, observability infrastructure) command premiums.

At the senior SDET level (5–8 years) at top companies, compensation reaches ₹45–80 lakh. Senior SDETs who have built test automation platforms used by multiple teams, who have demonstrably reduced production incident rates, or who have built performance testing capabilities from scratch are in this range. At MNC engineering centres in India — Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Atlassian, Adobe — senior SDETs earn ₹60–1 crore total compensation including equity.

The Staff and Principal SDET track — which exists at Microsoft, Google, and a few Indian companies like Freshworks — reaches ₹1–1.8 crore for engineers who are defining quality strategy at an organizational level, building internal testing platforms, and influencing engineering standards across hundreds of engineers.

## How to Maximize Your QA/SDET Compensation

The single highest-leverage action a QA/SDET engineer can take to improve their compensation is to move from manual testing skills to automation engineering skills, and from automation engineering to SDET-level programming and infrastructure skills. Each step roughly doubles the addressable salary range.

The second leverage point is company tier. An experienced SDET with 4 years of automation experience at TCS earns approximately ₹15–20 lakh. The same engineer, with the same 4 years of experience but at Freshworks or Razorpay, earns ₹28–40 lakh. The technical skills required to make this jump are significant but achievable in 4–6 months of deliberate preparation. The return on that investment is measured in the tens of lakhs over just the next 3–5 years.

Performance testing and security testing specializations carry explicit salary premiums. Performance engineers with JMeter, Gatling, or k6 expertise and the ability to interpret results at an architectural level are consistently offered 15–20% above generic SDET rates. Security testing engineers with application penetration testing skills (OWASP methodology, Burp Suite proficiency, secure code review) are in even shorter supply and command similar premiums.

## Global QA/SDET Salaries

For Indian SDET engineers who move abroad, the compensation uplift is significant.

In the United States, SDET roles at major tech companies pay $150,000–$200,000 total compensation at the mid-level, and $200,000–$350,000 at the senior level. Microsoft, Google, Apple, and Amazon are the largest SDET employers in the US and offer full RSU packages, comprehensive benefits, and annual performance bonuses. The interview bar for these roles is identical to SDE interviews — candidates must prepare with the same DSA and system design depth.

In the United Kingdom, senior SDET roles pay £75,000–£120,000 at leading tech companies. Germany and the Netherlands offer €70,000–€110,000. Singapore — which has a well-established tech sector with Grab, Shopee, Lazada, and sea of MNC regional offices — pays SGD $100,000–$180,000 for senior SDET roles.

# QA / SDET Tools and Tech Stack: The Complete Landscape for 2025

## UI Automation: The Transition from Selenium to Modern Tools

The UI automation landscape has changed significantly in the last three years, and understanding which tools are relevant in 2025 — as opposed to which tools dominate legacy job postings — is important for any SDET building their toolkit.

Selenium WebDriver has been the industry standard for browser automation for over 15 years. It is still present in the majority of QA job descriptions in India, particularly at enterprise companies, IT service companies, and any company whose automation suite predates 2021. Selenium's architecture — a client library that communicates with browser drivers via the WebDriver protocol — is the foundation that all modern browser automation tools are built on, which means understanding Selenium deeply also gives you conceptual fluency in every successor tool. You must know Selenium. But you should also understand its limitations: it requires explicit waits to handle asynchronous rendering, its test execution is slower than modern alternatives, and its debugging experience is poor compared to what Playwright and Cypress offer.

Playwright is the tool that most senior SDETs in India are migrating to for new projects. Developed by Microsoft and open-source, Playwright supports Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit in a single test run, offers auto-waiting that eliminates most flakiness from timing issues, provides network interception for mocking and stubbing API calls, and includes a powerful code generation tool that records browser interactions and generates test code. Playwright's API is available in TypeScript/JavaScript, Python, Java, and C#, giving it broad accessibility. For any new automation project started in 2024 or later, Playwright is the recommended choice.

Cypress is popular particularly at companies using JavaScript or TypeScript as their primary development stack. Its developer experience is excellent — the Cypress interactive test runner lets you see tests executing in real time with time-travel debugging — but its limitation is that it only supports Chromium-based browsers natively (Firefox support is partial and experimental). For teams where the primary browser target is Chrome and the team is JavaScript-first, Cypress is a strong choice. For teams that need cross-browser validation or prefer a language-agnostic approach, Playwright is more appropriate.

## API Testing: The Layer That Should Own Most of Your Coverage

API testing sits at the optimal level of the test pyramid for most applications: it is faster than UI tests, more reliable (no rendering or timing dependencies), and more precise (you are directly testing the contracts between services). Every SDET should have a strong API testing toolset.

Postman is the starting point and remains an essential part of every QA engineer's toolkit in India. Use it for API exploration, manual contract validation, and collaborative collection-sharing with your team. But Postman is not a substitute for automated API testing — its scripting capabilities are limited and its test code is not maintainable at scale. Use Postman to understand the API, then write your automated tests in a proper framework.

For Java-based teams, RestAssured is the industry standard. It provides a fluent DSL for writing API tests that are readable without being verbose, integrates naturally with JUnit and TestNG test runners, and has excellent support for JSON and XML response validation. The combination of RestAssured for API tests + TestNG for test management + Allure for reporting is the most common SDET stack at Indian product companies.

For Python-based teams, pytest combined with the requests library is the dominant approach. pytest is one of the best test frameworks in any language — its fixture system, parametrization, and plugin ecosystem are genuinely excellent. Python SDETs also frequently use httpx for async API testing and jsonschema for response schema validation.

GraphQL testing is an emerging requirement at companies that have adopted GraphQL APIs. GraphQL's query structure requires different validation approaches than REST — queries can be deeply nested, responses can vary based on the query structure, and schema validation requires GraphQL-aware tools. Learn to use Apollo Client for GraphQL testing, or the Postman GraphQL collection type for manual exploration.

## Mobile Testing: Appium and the Android/iOS Landscape

India is a mobile-first market, and quality engineers at consumer product companies inevitably encounter mobile testing. The dominant tool for mobile automation is Appium, which implements the WebDriver protocol for native iOS and Android applications.

Appium's architecture involves an Appium Server that translates WebDriver commands into native mobile automation (UIAutomator2 for Android, XCUITest for iOS), a test script written in any WebDriver-compatible language, and a real device or emulator/simulator running the application under test. The conceptual model is identical to Selenium for browsers — if you understand Selenium, Appium's architecture is immediately intuitive.

The practical challenges of mobile automation are significant: real device farms (BrowserStack, Sauce Labs, or your company's device lab) are expensive to maintain, emulators are fast but imperfect proxies for real device behavior, and mobile UI changes are frequent (OS updates regularly break locator strategies). These challenges are why mobile automation expertise is valued — it requires more engineering sophistication to maintain a reliable mobile automation suite than a web automation suite.

## CI/CD, Observability, and Quality Infrastructure

Jenkins remains the most common CI/CD tool in Indian enterprises and product companies. Know how to configure Jenkins pipelines (both scripted and declarative syntax), how to set up parallel test execution, how to configure webhooks for automatic test runs on pull request creation, and how to archive test artifacts. GitHub Actions has grown rapidly at startups and smaller product teams — it is simpler to configure than Jenkins and natively integrated with GitHub repositories. If you are joining a startup or a company with a modern DevOps culture, GitHub Actions knowledge is more immediately useful than Jenkins.

For test reporting, Allure Report is the standard in the Indian QA community. It generates detailed HTML reports from test execution data annotated with @Allure annotations in your test code, supports attachments (screenshots on failure, API request/response logs), and produces trend data across builds showing whether your test suite health is improving or degrading over time.

# Transitioning into QA / SDET: Complete Guide for Career Changers

## Manual QA to Automation SDET: The Most Common and Most Important Transition

The transition from manual testing to automation engineering is the most critical career move available to QA professionals in India today. It is the difference between a career that is slowly shrinking — as manual QA roles are automated away or offshored — and a career that is growing and commanding increasing compensation. Yet despite the urgency, most manual QA engineers delay this transition for years because it requires learning to code, and coding feels intimidating to someone who has spent their career in test management tools and bug trackers.

The intimidation is real but manageable. The programming skills required to become a competent automation engineer are not the same as those required to be a software developer. You do not need to know algorithms, system design, or full-stack development. You need to write clear, maintainable object-oriented code, understand test framework APIs, work with configuration files and environment variables, and be comfortable debugging code you wrote. For most people, this is achievable in 4–6 months with focused daily practice.

The recommended path: Start with Python. Python has the lowest syntax overhead of any language, the most readable code style, and an excellent ecosystem of testing libraries (pytest, requests, selenium, playwright-python). Complete a structured Python course (Automate the Boring Stuff with Python is free and excellent for the practical mindset). Then learn pytest deeply — fixtures, parametrization, plugins. Then build your first automation project against a real website. The transition from manual to automation is not a credential change — it is demonstrated by building something. Build it, put it on GitHub, and you are halfway to your first automation role.

The job search framing matters. Do not apply for "Manual QA" roles and then mention in your cover letter that you are learning automation. Apply for "Automation QA" or "SDET" roles at mid-market companies, lead with your automation project in your resume and cover letter, and acknowledge in interviews that you are making the transition with honesty and evidence. "I spent three years in manual QA at Infosys and have spent the last six months building an automation framework with Python and Playwright — here is the GitHub link" is a stronger narrative than pretending you are already an experienced SDET.

## Developer to SDET: An Underappreciated Career Path

Many software developers — particularly those who find themselves drawn to reliability, tooling, and developer experience problems — discover that the SDET role is a natural fit they had not considered. The SDET role leverages your programming skills fully while adding the testing dimension that gives your work a quality focus.

The knowledge gaps are smaller than most developers expect. Coming from a development background, you already have: programming proficiency (the hardest part of SDET for non-programmers), understanding of software architecture, familiarity with CI/CD pipelines, and the ability to read and understand production codebases. What you need to add: test design theory (equivalence partitioning, boundary value analysis, risk-based testing), automation framework design patterns (Page Object Model, test data management, test isolation), API testing specifics, and performance testing fundamentals.

The career pitch is powerful: an SDET who thinks like a developer — who understands the system they are testing at an architectural level, who can read and review the production code they are testing, and who can build testing tools that make developers more productive — is extremely valuable. Companies like Google (whose TEs and SDETs are often indistinguishable from SWEs), Microsoft, and Atlassian have built quality engineering cultures precisely because they understood that quality problems require engineering solutions.

## Non-Technical Backgrounds Entering QA

QA remains one of the most accessible entry points to the tech industry for people from non-CS backgrounds, and this accessibility is genuine — manual testing roles are learnable without programming prerequisites, and the structured thinking developed in other analytical careers often transfers directly to test design.

Business analysts, operations professionals, finance analysts, and customer support leads who have developed deep product knowledge are well-positioned to transition into QA. The skills that transfer: structured problem-solving, attention to detail, documentation discipline, and domain knowledge of the product. The skills that need to be built: understanding of software development lifecycle, familiarity with bug-tracking tools (JIRA), test case writing and management, and basic web/API literacy.

The entry point for a non-technical QA transition is almost always a domain-specific application of your existing knowledge. A healthcare professional entering QA at a healthtech startup, a financial analyst entering QA at a fintech company, or an educator entering QA at an edtech company brings domain context that engineering-background QAs lack. Companies value this — a QA who can identify that a feature design is clinically misleading, financially incorrect, or pedagogically unsound is catching a different and often more impactful class of defect than one who only validates technical correctness.

## First Week Action Plan in a New QA/SDET Role

Day 1–2: Set up your testing environment completely. For SDET roles, this means: clone the test repository, install all dependencies, understand the build system, run the existing test suite locally and confirm it passes. The state of the existing test suite tells you everything about the team's quality culture — a test suite with 30% pass rate, obsolete locators, and no documentation is telling you something important about where you will be spending your energy. Take notes.

Day 3: Read the last 20 failing bug reports in the bug tracker. Do not just read what failed — understand where in the system the defects originated, how long they lived before being caught, and whether they were caught by automated tests or manual testing or by users in production. This analysis gives you the quality risk landscape of the product faster than any onboarding presentation.

Day 4: Identify the three highest-risk areas of the product — the features with the most reported defects, the most complex business logic, or the most external integrations. These are where you should focus your early testing attention and where your first automation contributions will have the highest impact. Write down your hypothesis and share it with your team lead to validate it.

Day 5: Run the full CI pipeline from start to finish, including the test stage. Note which tests are slow, which are flaky (observe the test run twice if you can), and which parts of the system have low or no automated coverage. This investigation will become the foundation of your quality improvement roadmap for your first 90 days.

Week 2: Schedule individual 30-minute conversations with two developers on the team. Ask each one: "What part of the codebase are you most worried about from a quality perspective?" and "What would make your development process feel safer?" Developer answers to these questions reveal more about real quality risks than any test plan, and they immediately establish you as a quality partner rather than a quality gatekeeper — a distinction that will define how effectively you can do your job for as long as you are at this company.
